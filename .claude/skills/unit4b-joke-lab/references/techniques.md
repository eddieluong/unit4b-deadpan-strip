# Joke mechanics for UNIT 4B

Use these to generate the five-plus alternative punchlines per premise. Name the technique next to each
alternative so the scorecard can compare fairly.

## Structure
- **Setup → assumption → reversal.** The setup makes the audience assume X; the punchline reveals Y, which
  was also true all along. Write the assumption down first, then attack it.
- **Punch word last.** "I've buried men for less, dear" — not "For less, I've buried men".
- **Rule of three.** Two normal items, a third that breaks the pattern. In shorts the third is often silent
  (a picture instead of a line).
- **Tag.** A second, smaller laugh right after the first, on the same idea (the extra panel, Snuggles' aside).
- **Callback.** A line or image from earlier returns with a new meaning; strongest as the final beat.

## Character engines (built into the cast)
- **Normalized absurdity.** Treat the impossible as paperwork. Ted's death is a scheduling problem; Penny's
  age is irrelevant to the lease. The joke is the *mundane complaint*.
- **Sweet face, dark line.** The gentlest delivery carries the bleakest meaning (Nana, Penny). Use `fake_sweet`
  or `happy` with a threatening line.
- **Status flip.** The lowest-status character wins (an 8-year-old evicts an adult; a cat outwits everyone).
- **The straight man.** Steve says what the audience thinks. His reaction face sells the joke; he never jokes back well.

## Sarcasm toolkit (maps to the 10 sarcasm deliveries)
| Type | Pattern | Delivery |

| Fake excitement | "Yay! Paperwork!" | `mock_excited` |
| Fake kindness | "Bless your heart, you tried." | `fake_sweet` |

Sarcasm only lands if the literal meaning is obviously wrong in context. Show the context first.

## Dad jokes (Ted's specialty)
- Build: a question with an everyday word that has a ghost/death double meaning
  (boo/booze, spirit, grave/great, dead tired, coffin/coughing, eye-scream, boo-tiful, ghoul/goal).
- The pun must be obvious within 2 seconds to a non-native speaker. If you need to explain it, cut it.
- Ted delivers with `proud`; reactions are never laughter: Penny `deadpan` "No.", Steve `cringe`,
  Snuggles `side_eye`, Nana `deadpan` threat.

## Other reliable moves
- **Literal idiom.** "This job is killing me." → Ted: "Welcome. Meetings are Tuesdays."
- **Understatement.** A disaster described as a minor inconvenience (Nana: "Funny story, that's not tomato sauce.").
- **Hyperbole with a straight face.** Snuggles treats an empty bowl like a murder case.
- **Anti-climax.** Huge build-up, tiny reveal ("The hat was expensive.").
- **Specific numbers.** "Fifteen percent", "forty pages", "three goldfish": specific is funnier than vague.
- **Wrong priority.** The character cares about the wrong thing in a crisis (Penny worries about the clipboard, not the fire).

## Pacing in a short
Hook (0–1.5 s) → setup (≤ 12 s) → escalation → punch (70–85 %) → silent beat (0.8–1.6 s) → button (≤ 4 s) → optional loop.
