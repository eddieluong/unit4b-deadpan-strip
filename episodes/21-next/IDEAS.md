## 1. "Nine Hours" (weekly screen-time report)   score 35/40   [hook: PHONE]
- **Problem:** the phone sends you a report of how many hours you stared at the phone, and you look at it on the phone.
- **Glance frame 1:** the notification fills the top half in giant red digits "9h 41m"; Steve's face below it, eyes huge, one sweat drop.
  Caption: `POV: your screen time report`. One number, no reading needed (same family as the "1%" hook that gave stayed 47 %).
- **Mechanism (~28 s):**
  1. 0–3 s: the "9h 41m" banner drops; Steve: "That's a JOB." (flat horror)
  2. 3–8 s: vow: phone into a drawer, drawer closed, deep breath, birds; phone buzzes inside, he opens the drawer "to check the time".
  3. 8–13 s: limit set: "App limit: 30 min". Time-lapse (sun to moon, a spider builds a web on the drawer); banner "Limit reached".
  4. 13–17 s: PEAK (one short shout): he sees the new total "NINE HOURS?!" because he has been looking at the report.
  5. ~21 s PUNCH (flat, ~75–80 %): banner "You spent 22 min on this report"; Steve taps "Ignore limit": "…Ignore." (1 word, 2 with "Ignore limit")
  6. Button/loop: next-day banner "9h 52m"; Steve with the same face as frame 1. Loop.
- **Zero-lore check:** pass. Needs only Steve and a phone; every adult with a smartphone has seen the weekly report. Snuggles V.O. optional. No cat reveal.
- **Score 35:** surprise 4, clarity 5, fit 4, brevity 5, visual 5, hook 5, relatable 5, rewatch 4... weakest: surprise/fit (4; any sitcom could do the
  gag, Steve's deadpan carries the fit).
- **Risks:** looks like a sibling of One Percent (phone + number). Mitigate by making the object a *report* with a rising number, not a countdown.
  "Ignore limit" is a screen text, make it a big caption. No brand UI shown (generic OS card). Do not preach about phone addiction.
- **Evidence:** Pew 1 Sep 2026, 53 % say too much time ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/01/about-half-of-americans-say-they-spend-too-much-time-on-their-smartphone/));
  Ireland: 70 % say too much, 1 in 4 set limits ([IFSC/Deloitte](https://www.ifsc.ie/news/almost-one-in-four-people-in-ireland-have-set-screen-limits-on-their-devices-as-70-say-they-spend-too-much-time-on-their-phones)).

## 2. "Only 1 Left!" (fake scarcity tag)   score 33/40   [hook: NON-PHONE, physical shelf price tag]
- **Problem:** every product says "Only 1 left!" and you buy in a panic, then see the same tag on everything.
- **Glance frame 1:** a physical red shelf tag "ONLY 1 LEFT" (shop shelf, no screen; countdown is a store clock) in giant letters over a sweater; Steve's hand on it, face frozen mid-grab. Icon + number, readable in 0.2 s.
- **Mechanism (~28 s):**
  1. 0–3 s: Steve sees tag "ONLY 1 LEFT"; a countdown "00:09". He grabs it.
  2. 3–9 s: he races to the till against the store clock; a second shopper's hand (no face) reaches for the same sweater.
  3. 9–14 s: he wins; the next shelf shows the same sweater, tag "ONLY 1 LEFT", then the same on a hat, mug, socks (list x3 on ones).
  4. ~15 s PEAK: he buys all of them in a panic: "LAST ONE!" (short shout).
  5. ~22 s PUNCH (flat, ~77 %): the door opens, a mountain of identical boxes; each box labelled "1 of 40,000". Steve: "…Last one."
  6. Button/loop: Steve walks past the next shelf: a new tag "ONLY 1 LEFT"; he eyes it, steps back, steps in. Loop to frame 1.
- **Zero-lore check:** pass (shopping; no cast lore). Keep real brands and the word "Black Friday" off screen so it also plays after the sale week.
- **Score 33:** surprise 3, clarity 5, fit 3, brevity 5, visual 5, hook 5, relatable 5, rewatch 4 (weakest: fit and surprise 3; add Snuggles V.O.
  "Forty thousand last ones." to carry fit).
- **Risks:** 4/8 beats overlap Ep 15 (Early Black Friday, same season); frame 1 depends on a tag, not a face-led emotion; "boxes" punch is
  guessable. Never blame couriers or a real store. Timing is the strongest of the five (BF week).
- **Evidence:** BCG 4 Nov 2026: 1 in 5 shoppers distrust discounts, 54 % trust prices ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/press/4november2026-consumer-trust-black-friday-discounts-promotion-fatigue));
  53 % suspect fake low-stock claims ([Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/shoppers-dont-trust-black-friday-deals-heres-how-they-check-them));
  Omni Calculator 2026 US survey ([Omni](https://www.omnicalculator.com/reports/black-friday-survey-2026)).

## 3. "Unknown Number" (spam calls)   score 33/40   [hook: PHONE]   (kept from Tập 9 list)

  5. ~21 s PUNCH (flat, ~77 %): Steve: "…Sorry." (1 word)
  6. Button/loop: the call-back number shows "UNKNOWN" and rings; he declines out of reflex. Loop.
- **Zero-lore check:** pass (new voice for the caller = V.O. only; no cast needed).
- **Score 33:** surprise 3, clarity 5, fit 3, brevity 5, visual 4, hook 4, relatable 5, rewatch 4 (weakest: fit and surprise 3, "it was real" is a known twist).
- **Risks:** keep it nuisance calls, never fraud victims; the glance hook is a text label ("UNKNOWN"), a little less instant than a bare number; the

- **Evidence:** Hiya: 11 spam calls/month in the US vs 4 in the UK ([Hiya](https://blog.hiya.com/global-call-threat-report));

## 4. "On Empty" (fuel light)   score 32/40   [hook: NON-PHONE, car fuel gauge]
- **Problem:** the fuel light is on, the needle is on E, and you decide you can make it.
- **Glance frame 1:** a giant dashboard fuel gauge, needle pinned on "E", orange fuel-pump icon glowing; Steve's sweating face reflected in the glass (icon + face, no words).
- **Mechanism (~27 s):**
  1. 0–3 s: light "ding"; Steve: "Fine." He drives on.
  2. 3–9 s: x3 escalation: a station passes ("too pricey"), another ("too far"), a third ("I can make it"); each shot the needle sinks lower, Steve leans forward and whispers to the car.
  3. 9–14 s: the engine coughs; he coasts, doing the "lean the car forward" body sway; downhill = joy; silent third: he gets out and pushes it the last 10 m.
  4. ~15 s PEAK: he reaches the pump and kisses the nozzle: "I MADE IT!" (short shout).
  5. ~21 s PUNCH (flat, ~77 %): the attendant sets the nozzle in: the pump shows the tank already full, "LITRES: 0.0". Steve looks at the dashboard: needle still on E. "…It was full." (2–3 words)
  6. Button/loop: back in the car, the light dings again; Steve drives past the station. Loop.
- **Zero-lore check:** pass (Steve + car; works in any country; attendant is a nameless extra).
- **Score 32:** surprise 4, clarity 4, fit 3, brevity 5, visual 5, hook 5, relatable 4 (drivers only), rewatch 2 (weakest: rewatch 2; gauge-lie punch is quotable, button is the loop).
- **Risks:** drivers only (VN skews motorbikes; use a bike? keep a car to read the dashboard, or a scooter gauge if the owner prefers); "gauge is broken" needs a clear
  2 s showing the needle stuck; no dangerous driving shown, the engine just coughs.
- **Evidence:** Cazoo/Times survey: 39 % of drivers have "refuel anxiety" (62 % of Gen Z), date not confirmed ([Forecourt Trader](https://forecourttrader.co.uk/news/young-people-scared-of-petrol-stations-survey-says/708693.article));
  Motorpoint: 42 % wait for the fuel light before filling ([Motorpoint](https://motorpoint.co.uk/company-news/uk-motorists-will-see-nearly-400-added-to-fuel-bills-new-report-finds));
  1 in 8 drive on empty ([Scotsman](https://www.scotsman.com/news/uk-news/1-8-motorists-drive-empty-1628147)). Evergreen, no 2026 trend, UK-heavy.
