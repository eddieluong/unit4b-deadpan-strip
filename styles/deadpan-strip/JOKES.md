# UNIT 4B — Joke Playbook for Shorts

For an international, English-speaking audience on YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Reels. Read with `CAST.md` and `STYLE.md`.

## 1. Rules for every short

- **Length**: 20–40 s, one joke. A second joke only as an extra panel after the first lands.
- **Hook in the first 1.5 s**: open on the strangest image in the episode *and* a line that raises a question ("Who's Ted?", "Why is the thermostat gone?"). No logos, no intros, no establishing shots.
- **Punchline at 70–85 % of the runtime**, then a 0.8–1.6 s silent beat, then at most one button (reaction, extra panel or aside).
- **Loop the ending** when possible: the last frame or line flows back into the first, so the short replays itself.
- **Captions always**, burned in. Many viewers watch muted and many speak English as a second language.
- **Start from a real-life problem, never from the cast's lore.** Every short opens on an everyday annoyance the viewer has lived (a missed delivery, a rent hike, a broken Wi-Fi, a noisy neighbor). The joke must land for someone who has never seen another episode: if it only works when you already know Nana is a retired assassin or that Ted died in 4B, rewrite it so the screen shows what the viewer needs. Backstory may add a second layer, never carry the punchline.
- **Emotionalize it, but keep the person real.** The small everyday problem is a big deal *inside* the character, and the film shows that with visuals (a one-frame melt, a quick "what I pictured" cutaway, the greyscale drain, solemn music) and with Snuggles' noir narration. The character's voice stays the voice of a real tired adult: sighs, muttering, talking to themself, refreshing the app again. One short peak at most per short; the punchline is said flat ("...cool."). Nobody sobs or screams over a delivery; reference channels (TheOdd1sOut, Casually Explained, Jaiden, Domics) keep the voice conversational and put the exaggeration in the picture. See `research/viral/2026-10-23-channels/NOTES.md`.
- **Voices: emotional and natural, like a real person.** Not flat TTS, not theatre. Write lines the way people talk (contractions, "okay", trailing off with "..."), give each line one honest feeling (tired, pleased, annoyed, defeated) with a light v3 tag ([sighs], [mumbling to himself], [quietly], [exhales]), and render with stability ~0.35 (natural band, still expressive) and normal speed. Reserve stability 0.0 and shouting for the rare real peak.
- **Show, don't say — but don't go silent.** Cut a line only when it just describes what the picture already shows ("he refreshed the app forty-one times" over a wall of tally marks). Keep the lines that add something the image can't: attitude, irony, inner logic, the character's voice ("Nine to five. Easy." before a disaster; "He's close." right before the map shows stop 14 of 87; "Thirty seconds. I'll be thirty seconds."). Faces and props carry the facts; short muttered lines carry the personality. Typical short: 5–8 short lines.
- **Universal topics**: rent, landlords, roommates, Wi-Fi, work from home, bosses, dating apps, getting older, deliveries, smart homes, Mondays, adulting.
- **Language that survives subtitles**: short sentences, common words, idioms everyone knows (taken literally is fine). No puns that need a native ear, no local politics, slang, brands or celebrities.
- **Dark, never cruel**: death, ghosts, crime and money are fair game when they are absurd. Never punch down (race, religion, disability, real tragedies, real people). Violence is implied, never shown. Penny is never in danger; she is always the most powerful person in the room.

## 2. Formats that work in short video

| Format | How it works | Best cast |
|---|---|---|
| **Normalized absurdity** | Something impossible is treated as boring paperwork; the joke is the mundane complaint. | Ted, Penny |
| **Sweet face, dark line** | The gentlest-looking character says the darkest thing, calmly. | Nana, Penny |
| **The extra panel** | The scene ends, the beat holds, then one more panel reframes everything. | anyone |
| **Escalation ×3, third one silent** | Two spoken beats get worse; the third is a silent image. | Steve vs Ted |
| **Literal idiom** | Someone says a common idiom; someone else takes it literally. | Ted, Penny |
| **Anti-climax** | A huge build-up resolves into something tiny. | Snuggles, Steve |
| **Fourth-wall aside** | One character tells the viewer what is really going on; nobody else hears. | Snuggles |
| **Loop** | The final line is the first line again; the short restarts seamlessly. | Snuggles noir |
| **Dad joke + crushing reaction** | A proud, terrible pun; a silent beat; then the room's reaction is the real joke. The worse the pun, the better. | Ted tells, Penny kills it, Nana threatens |
| **Sarcastic one-liner** | A long, earnest line answered by two or three dry words. | Ted, Penny, Snuggles |

**Titles**: name the situation, not the joke. "POV: your landlord is 8", "When the previous tenant never left", "My neighbor is 78 and terrifying". Keep under 50 characters.

## 3. Faces that sell the joke

The reaction face is half the punchline. Typical pairings (expression names from `EXPRESSIONS`, ElevenLabs mode in brackets):

| Beat | Teller's face | Reaction faces |
|---|---|---|
| Dad joke | Ted `proud` [dad_joke] | Penny `deadpan` [unimpressed], Steve `cringe`, Snuggles `side_eye` |
| Dark line from a sweet face | Nana `happy` → `evil` on the extra panel [deadpan_threat] | Steve `horrified` then `dead_inside` |
| Absurd fact as paperwork | Penny `deadpan` [corporate] | Steve `shocked` → `nervous` |
| Sarcastic one-liner | Ted `deadpan` [dry_sarcasm] | Steve `confused` |
| Fourth-wall aside | Snuggles `smug` [sarcastic_aside] | nobody: the others stay frozen in their last face |
| Kid slip | Penny `happy` or `laughing` [kid_slip] | everyone `shocked`; Penny snaps back to `deadpan` |

Rule: hold the reaction face through the silent beat; change it once, small, to land the laugh.

## 4. Visual gags built into the designs

| Character | Built-in gag | Joke it generates |
|---|---|---|
| Steve | the ever-present sweat drop | the one time he isn't sweating, everyone panics |
| Nana Knuckles | the beehive holds things; magnified eyes | she pulls an alarming object out of her hair; a tiny side-eye looks enormous |
| Penny | a giant serious head on a tiny body | corporate gravitas vs. not reaching the doorbell |
| Ted | floating pink slippers | the slippers lag behind, get lost, get stolen by Snuggles |
| Snuggles | a fedora two sizes too small; a tough-guy name like Snuggles | noir monologue from a cat called Snuggles; the hat falls off at the worst moment |

## 5. Running gags (use one per episode at most)

- Ted and the thermostat (he likes it cold; he is dead).
- Nana Knuckles's unexplained skills and her hip ("Mostly my hip.").
- Penny's corporate jargon vs being eight (juice boxes, bedtime, nap clauses in the lease).
- Steve apologizing to everyone, including the ghost and the cat.
- Snuggles's noir monologues about tuna.
- Ted's dad jokes (all ghost puns, all from 1987) and Penny's flat "No." after every one.

## 6. Sample scripts

Timing is for a 9:16 short. `[beat]` = silent hold, no caption, no music.

### 01 · The Listing  (normalized absurdity + extra panel, ~32 s)
1. **0:00** Medium two-shot, doorway. PENNY (clipboard): "Natural light. Original floors. Very quiet neighbors."
2. **0:05** Ted drifts through the wall behind Steve with his mug. Steve doesn't notice.
3. **0:08** STEVE: "Wow. And the rent?" PENNY: "Twelve hundred. Ted is included."
4. **0:13** STEVE: "Who's Ted?" `[beat 1.2 s]` slow push-in; Ted sips, right behind him.
5. **0:18** TED: "Hello." Steve jumps on a 2-frame smear, mouth wide.
6. **0:22** Extra panel: Penny writing. PENNY: "Tenant screams. Deposit is non-refundable." Snuggles, aside: "They all scream. The deposit never comes back."

### 02 · The Thermostat  (escalation ×3, silent third, ~25 s)
1. **0:00** Insert: thermostat reads 16°. Steve turns it to 22°. STEVE: "There."
2. **0:05** Cut, same framing, next morning: 16° again. Ted floats beside it, sipping. STEVE: "Ted." TED: "I run warm."
3. **0:12** STEVE: "You're dead." TED: "And yet."
4. **0:16** Cut, same framing, third morning: the thermostat is gone, a neat hole in the wall. `[beat 1.4 s]`
5. **0:20** Wide: Ted hugs the thermostat in his armchair like a teddy bear. No words. Music sting.

### 03 · Walking Home  (sweet face, dark line, ~22 s)
1. **0:00** Stairwell. STEVE: "Nana Knuckles, you shouldn't walk home alone at night. It's dangerous."
2. **0:05** NANA (smiling): "Oh, it is, dear."
3. **0:08** STEVE: "Right. So, you'll be careful?" NANA: "I wasn't talking about me." `[beat 1.2 s]`, Steve's eyes slide to Nana. She keeps smiling.
4. **0:15** Extra panel, Snuggles aside: "Three muggers left this neighborhood last year. They left their shoes, too."

### 04 · The Complaint  (anti-climax + kid boss, ~28 s)
1. **0:00** Penny's "office": a desk far too big for her. STEVE: "Penny. The sink leaks, the heater is haunted, and there's a cat in a hat living in the vents."
2. **0:08** PENNY folds her sleeves: "I hear your concerns." STEVE: "Thank you."
3. **0:12** PENNY: "Your rent is going up ten percent." STEVE: "For what?!"
4. **0:15** `[beat 1 s]` PENNY: "The hat was expensive."
5. **0:19** Pull back: Snuggles in the vent opening, fedora tilted. SNUGGLES, to camera: "Worth it."

### 05 · 3 A.M.  (fourth-wall noir + loop, ~30 s)
1. **0:00** Dark kitchen, fridge light. SNUGGLES (V.O.): "Apartment 4B. Three a.m. Somebody's in the kitchen."
2. **0:06** Ted stares into the open fridge at a block of cheese.
3. **0:10** TED (whisper): "I can't taste it anymore. I just like to look."
4. **0:15** SNUGGLES (V.O.): "Every night, same thing. He looks at the cheese. I look at him."
5. **0:21** `[beat]` The cheese, insert. SNUGGLES: "And the cheese... looks back."
6. **0:26** Fridge closes, black. SNUGGLES: "Apartment 4B. Three a.m." (loops to the first shot)

### 06 · Dying Here  (literal idiom, ~18 s)
1. **0:00** Steve on the couch, on the phone. STEVE: "Mom, this job is killing me. I'm literally dying here."
2. **0:05** Ted floats past with his mug, doesn't stop. TED: "Welcome. Meetings are on Tuesdays."
3. **0:09** `[beat 1 s]` STEVE (to phone): "I'll call you back."
4. **0:12** Extra panel: a sign-up sheet on the fridge, "GHOST SUPPORT GROUP — TUESDAYS", one name: Ted. Below it, in Penny's handwriting: "Room fee: $20."

### 07 · For the Boos  (dad joke + crushing reaction, ~24 s)
Voice file: `voice-samples/ep07_dad_joke.lines.json` (ElevenLabs modes in brackets).
1. **0:00** Kitchen. TED (`dad_joke`, setup, no chuckle): "Steve. Why did the ghost go to the party?"
2. **0:03** STEVE (`nervous`): "Please don't."
3. **0:05** TED (`dad_joke`): "For the boos." *(chuckles)* "Boos." `[beat 1 s]`
4. **0:10** Hard cut, Penny in the doorway. PENNY (`unimpressed`): "No."
5. **0:12** Nana at the table, still smiling. NANA (`deadpan_threat`): "Ted, dear. I've buried men for less."
6. **0:16** TED (`dry_sarcasm`): "I'm already dead, Nana." NANA (`menace_whisper`): "Then I'll be very thorough."
7. **0:21** Extra panel, Snuggles (`cat_pun`): "In this building, the jokes die. The ghost stays."

**Dad joke rules**: the pun must be obvious within 2 seconds for a non-native speaker (boo/booze, grave/great, spirit). Ted is always proud of it. The reaction is never laughter.

## 7. Writing checklist

Before delivering any video, also run the full review in `QA.md` (lessons from Tập 1 and the hook / length / framing / sound checks).

- Can a 13-year-old in Brazil reading the captions get it? If not, simplify.
- Is the first frame strange enough to stop a thumb?
- Is there exactly one moment of silence, right before or after the punchline?
- Does every character sound like only themselves? Swap the names: if the line still works for someone else, rewrite it.
- Would the last frame make sense as the first frame? If yes, loop it.
