# How big should the emotion be? Reference channels (2026-10-23)

Question from the user after ep 10 v2: Steve's emotion was too big ("nobody reacts like that to a late delivery").
We pulled the 30 most-viewed shorts of TheOdd1sOut, Casually Explained, Pencilmation, Haminations, Jaiden Animations
and Domics (157 shorts, `candidates.md`). Subtitles could not be downloaded this time: YouTube rate-limited the
machine (IpBlocked / HTTP 429). The notes below come from titles, view counts and the known style of these channels;
re-run `subs` on the top ids later to confirm with transcripts.

## What the top shorts are about
Small personal moments, told as "this happened to me": wanting to pet a dog (55M), "I'm so in shape" (43M),
cracking a tooth (39M), "I can't catch a break" (20M), the dentist (7.9M), a restaurant out of meat (8.8M),
a date disaster (11M), trying beer for the first time (9M), "my toxic trait" (12M). No epic stakes, all ordinary.

## How they handle emotion (the calibration we got wrong)
1. **The voice stays real.** Storytime channels speak like a friend telling you what happened: conversational,
   a little dry, self-deprecating. Peaks are short (one "WHAT?!", one groan), never sustained sobbing or screaming.
   Casually Explained goes further: flat monotone over absurd honesty.
2. **The exaggeration lives in the picture, not the performance.** A face that melts for one frame, a quick
   imagined cutaway ("what I pictured" vs "what happened"), a tiny drawing of the inner thought. Then straight back.
3. **The joke is the honest, specific detail.** "I didn't shower in case he came" lands because it's true and
   specific, not because it is shouted.
4. **Reactions are the ones real people have:** a sigh, a pause, muttering, a small disbelieving laugh,
   talking to yourself, checking the tracking app for the fifth time.
5. **Understatement at the punch.** The bigger the disaster, the flatter the line ("cool. cool cool cool.").

## Rule for UNIT 4B
Inside: a drama. Outside: a normal person. Steve reacts like a real tired adult (sighs, mutters, refreshes the app);
the emotional size shows in short visual cutaways and in Snuggles' noir narration, never in Steve screaming or
sobbing. One short peak at most per short, and the punchline is said flat.
