## Patterns
1. **The voice carries the video.** Reference storytime/explainer shorts talk 2.2–3.7 words a second, almost without gaps;
   the drawings illustrate what is being said. We talk 1.2–1.3 words/s and leave the picture to carry long silent
   stretches — exactly what stiff animation can't do. The two exceptions (14–18 s pure sight gags) are very short.
2. **The first sentence states the premise in plain words, in the first 3 s**, usually first person: "My toxic trait is…",
   "One time…", "I need to… but…", "What people get wrong is…". It promises a story and a judgement.
3. **First person, told to the viewer.** The narrator is the person it happened to (or an opinionated explainer), so
   every line carries attitude. Our split (cat narrator in 3rd person + Steve muttering) halves the attitude per line.
4. **A joke every 5–8 s, not one joke per video.** Lists and escalations ("X means…, Y means…") put a laugh in each sentence;
   storytime shorts add small asides between beats ("as many of you know, I look much younger than I am").
5. **The ending reframes or tops the premise** (rule of two/three, self-own, a quote that becomes the title).
6. **Specific, true-sounding details** ("three large lemonades", "lava monster", "24 at a hibachi restaurant").

## What this means for UNIT 4B
Write the next scripts as **voice-led** shorts: one opinionated voice talking ~2.5 words/s from second 0, a premise in the
first sentence, a small laugh every 5–8 s, a reframing ending; the stiff pictures illustrate each sentence (a new drawing
per sentence) instead of acting it out. Keep silence for one beat only (before the punch).
